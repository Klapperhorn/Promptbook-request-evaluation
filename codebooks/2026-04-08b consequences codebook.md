## Code: It decreased trust in political actors or institutions.
Description:  
Claims that the energy crisis and/or political responses lowered trust and legitimacy of political actors or political institutions.

Inclusion criteria:
- Mentions declining trust/support, growing skepticism, disappointment, or perceived inefficacy of politics/institutions.
- Evaluates political handling as insufficient or conflicted by interests.
    
Exclusion criteria:
- Mentions cooperation problems without blaming actors
- Expresses only personal anxiety

Included examples:
- die EU verliert an Unterstützung und wird eher hinterfragt.
- Die Politiker haben die Menschen völlig vergessen
- Hier hat die komplette EU versagt

## Code: It benefited rather right-leaning parties

Description:  
Claims that the energy crisis and/or political responses increased support for right-leaning parties or lowered left-leaning support.

Inclusion criteria:
- Explicitly references rightward shift, right-wing parties, or electoral gains for the right as a consequence of dissatisfaction.
- Expresses benefits for right-leaning politics (e.g., nationalist, weaken EU, lower support for migrants and global south)
    
Exclusion criteria:
- General distrust in politics without a partisan direction
- Mentions polarization/splitting without specifying right-leaning gains    
- Mentions manipulation or democratic threat without party direction
    
Included examples:
- “...die Energiekrise einer von vielen Triggern für den Rechtsruck in Europa”
- Die Grünen/Linken mussten es ausbaden

## Code: It benefited rather left-leaning parties

Description:  
Claims the crisis/policy responses increased support for left-leaning parties (e.g., social democrats, greens, left parties) or lowered support for right-wing politics.

Inclusion criteria:
- Explicitly states left-leaning parties gained support or that voters shifted left due to the crisis.
- Attributes left gains to preferences for social protection, price caps, redistribution, or stronger state intervention.
    
Exclusion criteria:
- Mentions climate/renewables attitudes without electoral implications
- General dissatisfaction with “politics” without left direction
- Mentions only policy content (e.g., price cap) without indicating party support changes.
    
Included examples:
- Die Grünen/Linken profitieren von der Krise

## Code: It has negatively impacted industrial production in the EU.

Description:  
Attributes macro- or sector-level economic decline to the energy crisis/political responses, including recession risk, weakened industry, investment uncertainty, or sectoral damage.

Inclusion criteria:
- Mentions economy-wide harm (e.g., “Wirtschaft hat gelitten”, “downturn”, recession).
- Mentions sector impacts (automotive, pharmacies/supply chains, food production) as evidence of broader economic damage.
- Mentions uncertainty/contracting issues that imply economic slowdown or investment paralysis.
    
Exclusion criteria:
- Pure household cost pressure without broader economic framing
- Personal worries about finances without explicit economic-level claims
- Energy-transition debate without economic impact

Included examples:
- “Veel onzekerheid als je een nieuw energiecontract moest afsluiten…”
- “Vor allem die deutsche Wirtschaft macht mir Sorgen.”
- “Die Wirtschaft hat stark gelitten, etwa die Autoindustrie…”

## Code: It slowed down the energy transition

Description:  
States the crisis/political responses slowed down or reduced public support for renewable energy transition and/or climate policy, including backlash against climate measures and goals.

Inclusion criteria:
- Explicitly links the crisis to reduced support for climate protection, renewables, or the transition.
- Mentions disputes around transition policies (including nuclear phase-out debates) as undermining acceptance.
- Frames climate/transition as deprioritized due to inflation/cost concerns or crisis pressures.
    
Exclusion criteria:
- General mention of climate/environment topics without indicating decreased support.
- Preference shift toward nuclear without broader “less support for renewables/transition” claim

Included examples:
- “Inflation, Klima- und Umweltschutz und gesellschaftliche Debatten”
- “Der Streit über das Abschalten der Atomkraftwerke hat die Stimmung angeheizt.”

## Code: It accelerated energy transition

Description:
States the crisis/political responses increased support or sped up the energy transition: more renewables, electrification, efficiency, and modernization measures (e.g., PV, insulation, heat pumps)

Inclusion criteria:
- Explicit acceleration/increase in transition actions (more renewables, investment, subsidies, faster shift).
- Claims that crisis results in more PV/solar, wind, heat pumps, insulation/building modernization, efficiency programs.
- Mentions renewables installation as response to the crisis
- Claims that the crisis incentivised to reduce fossil fuel dependence.

Exclusion criteria:
- Pure statements about import diversification without renewables/transition/efficiency component
- Mentions of policies without an outcome/acceleration claim.

Included Examples
- “erneuerbare Energien ausgebaut”
- “Erneuerbare Energien wurden stärker gefördert”
- “mehr in erneuerbare Energien investiert”
- “Solaranlagen, Wärmepumpen, Dämmung … subventioniert”

Inclusion criteria:
- Mentions that one of these threats to democracy increased or contributed to the crisis

Exclusion criteria:

- Purely personal pessimism without democracy claim

Included Examples

- Pro-Russian policymakers continue to claim that global warming is a hoax.

- Frames energy-saving behavior as voluntary/proactive rather than forced by costs.

## Code: It exposed infrastructure weaknesses

Description:
States that the crisis revealed vulnerabilities or capacity limits in the EU/national energy infrastructure (especially the electricity grid), highlighting fragility, bottlenecks, or need for expansion.

Inclusion criteria:
- Explicit mention of grid/infrastructure limits or bottlenecks (overload, congestion, risk of blackout, insufficient capacity, “not resilient”).
 - Crisis framed as “revealing/showing/teaching/learning” about weakness, decay, or lack of robustness of the energy system.
- Mentions increased acceptance/urgency for grid expansion, reinforcement, or maintanance.
- Mentions socio-technical energy trade and distribution systems as insufficient.

Exclusion criteria:
- Supplier/import dependency without infrastructure focus (use High import dependency).
- Pure policy evaluation (handled well/badly) without an infrastructure weakness claim.
- Statements only about prices/costs without infrastructure capacity/vulnerability.

Included examples:
- “elektriciteitsnet … overbelast”
- “Ausbau des Energienetzes”
- “gezeigt wie abhängig wir sind”
