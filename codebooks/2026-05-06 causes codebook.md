## Code: EU political actors

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on EU-level political actors, institutions, decisions, coordination failures, or policy frameworks, such as the EU, Brussels, EU institutions, EU sanctions, EU directives, the Green Deal, EU market rules, or politicians acting in an EU role.

Inclusion criteria:

- The EU, Brussels, the European Commission, European Parliament, European Council, or “Europe” is blamed as a political actor.
- EU-level decisions or failures are framed as causing, worsening, or prolonging the crisis.
- The statement refers to EU sanctions, the Green Deal, EU directives, EU market rules, or other EU policy frameworks.
- The statement blames lack of a unified European response, weak coordination between member states, disunity, or failed consultations between European countries.
- The statement blames failure to diversify energy sources at the European level.
- A named politician is blamed in an EU role, for example as a Commissioner, MEP, or Brussels politician.
- “European politicians” or similar wording clearly refers to the supranational level.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only a member-state government, party, ministry, parliament, or national politician is blamed.
- “The government” or “governments” is mentioned without a clear EU-level cue.
- The EU is mentioned only as affected by the crisis, not as the actor causing or worsening it.
- The statement focuses on national planning, infrastructure, maintenance, investment, or domestic energy policy without a clear EU frame.
- Only abstract ideas such as greed, incompetence, or corruption are blamed without a clear EU actor or EU policy.
- Only a non-EU foreign actor is blamed.

Included examples:

- “Sanktionen gegen Russland”
- “Green Deal heeft de crisis verergerd”
- “Brüssel hat versagt…”
- “les sanctions de l’UE”
- “Lack of a strong, unified energy policy.”
- “Too few consultations between European countries.”
- “The EU was not prepared for the cutoff of Russian gas.”

## Code: National political actors

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on national-level political actors in EU member states, such as governments, ministries, parliaments, parties, or national politicians. Use this as the fallback category when political blame is present but there is no clear EU-level cue.

Inclusion criteria:

- A national government, ministry, parliament, party, or politician is blamed as causing, worsening, or prolonging the crisis.
- A national policy or policy failure is framed as causal, such as poor planning, delayed action, underinvestment, lack of maintenance, domestic production cuts, or weak infrastructure.
- A named politician is blamed in a national role.
- A national party is blamed as a political actor.
- “The government” is mentioned without an EU cue.
- “Governments” is mentioned, but the statement does not clearly refer to EU institutions, Brussels, EU policy, or collective European coordination.
- Generic political blame, such as politicians, rulers, leaders, or those in charge, is coded here unless the statement clearly points to the EU level.
- A specific member state, such as Germany, Poland, or France, is blamed for its own policy choices.

Exclusion criteria:

- The blamed actor is clearly the EU, Brussels, an EU institution, EU sanctions, EU rules, or failure of European coordination.
- The statement focuses on a unified European response, supranational policy, or Europe-wide dependence as the main causal issue.
- Only abstract ideas such as greed or incompetence are blamed without a clear political actor.
- Only a non-EU foreign political actor is blamed.
- The statement mentions the EU only as part of the context, while the actual blamed actor is national.
- The statement blames media actors or economic actors without critique of their political regulation.

Included examples:

- “Was die Bundesregierung beschlossen hat”
- “Merkel … ohne Weitblick …”
- “weil sich Deutschland überall einmischt”
- “reducing national gas production”

## Code: Industry and market actors

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on companies, suppliers, producers, traders, utilities, or market actors whose decisions, strategies, pricing, lobbying, or profit-seeking behavior caused or worsened the crisis.

Inclusion criteria:

- Energy companies, utilities, oil and gas firms, or market actors are explicitly blamed.
- Pricing behavior, profiteering, speculation, underinvestment, procurement decisions, or lobbying are framed as causal.

Exclusion criteria:

- The statement only judges how the actor responded to the crisis, without clearly presenting that response as causing, worsening, or prolonging it.
- Only political actors blamed.
- Companies only described as affected.
- The statement blames capitalism or greed only in the abstract without any concrete company or industry actor.
- War/Russia mentioned without linking to corporate/industry behavior

Included examples:

- “Geldgier der Energieversorger”
- caused by price speculation
- “Energiefirmen … bereichert”
- “Gewinnmaximierung im Ölmarkt”

## Code: Media actors

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on media organizations, social media platforms, or media-driven information dynamics that worsen the crisis through panic, misinformation, amplification, agenda-setting, or pressure effects.

Inclusion criteria:

- News media, media discourse, or social media are explicitly blamed as a causal driver, worsening the situation.
- Mechanism is informational: disinformation/rumors, sensationalism, fear/panic/stockpiling, agenda/pressure effects.

Exclusion criteria:

- Media is discussed as a social problem (kids/media use) with no link to their content.
- The statement only judges how the media responded to the crisis, without clearly presenting that response as causing, worsening, or prolonging it.
- Media is just mentioned as a (neutral) source of information.
- Radicalization, misinformation, or polarization is mentioned without media being the explicit causal actor.

Included examples:

- “Medien panik …”
- Die Bildzeitung gehört für diese Hetzkampagne nach wie vor irgendwie bestraft

## Code: Russia's war on Ukraine

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on the war/invasion/attack relating to Ukraine as a cause.

Inclusion criteria:

- Ukraine war explicitly cited as the driver of higher prices/shortages.
- Russia/Putin’s wartime actions are described as causing the energy crisis.

Exclusion criteria:

- High import is discussed without war/invasion cues.
- “Wars” or “conflicts” (plural) are mentioned generically without reference to Ukraine or Russia.
- Sanctions are blamed but war/invasion is not mentioned
- Ukraine war is explicitly considered as a false cause.

Included examples:

- “Wegen des Ukrainekriegs…”
- “Der russische Überfall…”
- "seit dem Krieg ein Engpass"
- “Putin und sein Vorgehen gegen die Ukraine.”

## Code: Too-fast energy transition

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on a too-fast energy transition, especially excessive reliance on renewables.

Inclusion criteria:

- Renewables, greening, or electrification are presented as causing or worsening the crisis.
- The transition is described as too fast, premature, unrealistic, or destabilizing.
- Blames Green energy politics.

Exclusion criteria:

- Weather or natural conditions are blamed without criticizing transition policy.
- Renewables are mentioned only as a solution, not as the cause.

Included examples:

- "Te snel overstappen van fossiele brandstoffen naar puur electrich"

## Code: Dependence on fossil fuels

Description:
Blames the energy crisis on reliance on fossil fuels, delayed transition away from them, or dependence on imported energy and external supply.

Inclusion criteria:

- Dependence on gas, oil, coal, or fossil energy is framed as causing or worsening the crisis.
- The statement blames slow transition to renewables, electrification, or related infrastructure.
- The statement refers to dependence on imports, foreign energy, outside suppliers, lack of energy independence, lack of internal production, or lack of alternative sources in a way that implies energy or fuel dependence.
- The statement blames reliance on one source or lack of diversification, especially when gas, oil, imports, or external supply are mentioned or implied.

Exclusion criteria:

- The statement blames only one specific supplier, such as Russia, without broader energy or import dependence.
- The main cause is war, sanctions, sabotage, or another external shock, unless fossil, fuel, or import dependence is also stated.
- The statement blames a too-fast move away from fossil fuels.
- The statement mentions foreign dependence or self-sufficiency, but not clearly in relation to energy, fuel, imports, or supply.

Included examples:

- “Gaslastiges Energiesystem”
- "auf Billig Gas und Öl Preis gesetzt"
- "We waren te afhankelijk van fossiele brandstoffen"
- “lentidão em medidas para mais energias renováveis”
- “Relying too much on other countries”
- “Having to buy raw materials abroad”
- “relying on a single source (supplier) of gas and oil”
- “Europe depends on foreign energy supply”

## Code: Concentrated energy import dependency

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on the dependence on one or a few external energy suppliers, source countries, or import channels, or to failure to diversify imports.

Inclusion criteria:

- Dependence on imported energy from one or few countries or suppliers is explicitly blamed.
- Lack of diversification, overexposure to Russian gas, or being too tied to a supplier is presented as causal.
- Mechanism is concentration risk: one supplier cuts/restricts supply; “too tied to” a specific source.

Exclusion criteria:

- Russia is blamed purely via war framing.
- Only blames general fossil energy dependence without specific supplier concentration.

Included examples:

- “Die EU hing zu sehr am russischen Gas”
- "Dat we gas moesten halen uit landen zoals Rusland"
- “Russische Gaslieferungen”
- "Verknappung der Energie aus den importländern"
- “Merkel hat sich zu sehr auf Putin verlassen”

## Code: Uncontrollable natural disasters

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on natural or environmental events, such as extreme cold, drought, flood, heat, low water levels, or other weather-related disruptions.

Inclusion criteria:

- A specific natural event is named and treated as the causal driver.
- Attributions to the climate change
- The framing is nature/unavoidable rather than policy failure.
- Unexpected weather events caused energy shortage.

Exclusion criteria:

- Weather is mentioned only as evidence that renewables are unreliable; code transition critique instead.
- Climate ideology or climate policy is blamed rather than physical environmental events.
- Covid-19 or pandemics

Included examples:

- “extremer Winter …”
- "Klimaprobleme"
- “Viele französische Atomkraftwerke waren vom Netz” (due to weather events, not political agenda)

## Code: External shocks and technical failures

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on discrete external shocks or technical failures, such as pandemics, wars outside Ukraine, geopolitical conflicts, trade wars, or infrastructure breakdowns.

Inclusion criteria:

- COVID-19 or pandemic-related disruption is explicitly blamed.
- The cause is a discrete event or failure rather than a broad social trend.
- Grid failure, power plant breakdown, pipeline malfunction, or infrastructure failure is explicitly blamed.
- Non-Ukraine wars, trade wars, or geopolitical conflicts are explicitly blamed (including Netanyahu / Israel, Palestine, Trump / USA).

Exclusion criteria:

- blames generalized trends, e.g. rising consumption
- blames an abstract ideology or social mentality.
- blames specific actors or structural energy dependence.
- blames EU or national policies, such as sanctions.
- blames Russia’s war on Ukraine or Nordstream sabotage.
- blames natural or environmental environemntal disasters.

Included examples:

- “Corona Pandemic …”
- Vooral de gaslevering kwam onder druk door internationale conflicten en oorlogen
- De verschillende oorlogen
- "the failure of French nuclear power plants"

## Code: Abstract societal and ideological explanations

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on abstract ideologies, system logics, societal tendencies, or broad global trends rather than on a specific actor, institution, or event. This includes capitalism, market logic, green ideology, greed, irrationality, false beliefs, short-term thinking, population growth, rising consumption, or rising energy demand.

Inclusion criteria:

- The cause is abstract, generalized, or diffuse and not linked to a concrete actor.
- A worldview, value system, social tendency, or system logic is framed as causing or worsening the crisis.
- The crisis is blamed on broad moral or cognitive failings, such as greed, irrationality, false beliefs, or short-term thinking, without naming a responsible actor, such as the EU or national politicians.
- The crisis is blamed on generalized macro-trends, such as population growth, rising consumption, or rising energy demand.

Exclusion criteria:

- A concrete actor is blamed, such as the EU, national governments, parties, companies, Russia, or energy suppliers.
- A discrete event or shock is blamed, such as war, COVID, or technical failure.
- Ideology, greed, or poor planning is attributed to a specific actor, e.g. Brussels, the Greens, our government, or energy companies.
- A specific structural energy mechanism is blamed, such as fossil dependence or import dependency.
- The text sounds ideological but does not clearly present ideology or society as the cause.

Included examples:

- “grüne Ideologie / Klimawahn”
    
- “Kapitalismus / neoliberale Marktwirtschaft”
    
- “Gier / Profitlogik”
    
- “das System / der Markt”
    
- grotere weredlpopulatie

# Code: Insufficient EU coordination and communication

Description:  
Blames the EU for insufficient comunication or coordination of member states to cause or worsen the the Energy crisis.

Inclusion criteria:

- EU blamed for bad coordination of member states, e.g., leading to price hikes.
- EU blamed for an inconsistent or slow response to the emerging crisis.
- EU blamed for ignoring signs of the emerging crisis.
- EU blamed for insufficient communication efforts with other EU countries.

Exclusion criteria:

- General blame of the EU and EU policies, such as the Green deal.
- EU blamed for other reasons that coordination and communication efforts.

Included examples:

- The EU's lack of decisive action
- Poor communication of the EU with other states
