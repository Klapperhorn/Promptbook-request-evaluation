## Code: EU political actors

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on EU-level political actors, institutions, decisions, coordination failures, or policy frameworks.

Inclusion criteria:

- Blames the EU, Brussels, the European Commission, European Parliament, European Council, or “Europe” as a political actor.
- Blames “European politicians” or a named politician in an EU role, for example as a Commissioner, MEP, or Brussels politician.
- Blames EU-level decisions, failures, policies, and actions, such as EU sanctions, the Green Deal, EU directives, EU market rules, or other EU policy framework for causing, worsening, or prolonging the crisis.
- Blames lack of a unified European response, weak coordination between member states, disunity, or failed consultations between European countries.
- Blames failure to diversify energy sources or to prepare for the crisis at the European level.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames a member-state government, party, ministry, parliament, or national politician.
- Only blames “The government” or “governments” without a clear EU-level cue.
- The EU is only mentioned as affected by the crisis, not as causing/worsening actor.
- Only blames national planning, infrastructure, maintenance, investment, or domestic energy policy without a clear EU-level cue.
- Only blames abstract ideas such as greed, incompetence, or corruption are blamed without a concrete EU actor or policy.
- Only a non-EU foreign actor is blamed.

Included examples:

- “Sanktionen gegen Russland”
- “Green Deal heeft de crisis verergerd”
- “Brüssel hat versagt…”
- “les sanctions de l’UE”
- “Lack of a strong, unified energy policy.”
- “Too few consultations between European countries.”
- “The EU was not prepared for the cutoff of Russian gas.”
- "Europe's inability to diversify energy sources"

## Code: National political actors

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on national-level political actors in EU member states. Use this as the fallback category when political blame is present but there is no clear EU-level cue.

Inclusion criteria:
- A national policy or policy failure is framed as causal, such as poor planning, delayed action, underinvestment, lack of maintenance, high taxes, domestic production cuts, nuclear phase-out, or weak infrastructure.
- Blames a national government, ministry, parliament, party, or politician for causing, worsening, or prolonging the crisis.
- Blames an EU member state for their policy choices.
- Blames a named politician in the national role or a political party.
- Blames “the government” (singular) or “governments” without explicit EU cue.
- Blames politics generically, in terms of politicians, rulers, leaders, or those in charge, unless the statement clearly points to the EU level.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames the EU, Brussels, an EU institution, EU sanctions, EU rules, or failure of European coordination.
- Only blames the lack of a unified European response, supranational policy, or Europe-wide dependence as the main causal issue.
- Only blames abstract ideas such as greed or incompetence without a clear political actor.
- Only blames non-EU political actors.
- Only blames media actors or economic actors without critique of political regulation.
- Only blames a generic "they" without implicit critique of national political actors.

Included examples:

- “Was die Bundesregierung beschlossen hat”
- “Merkel … ohne Weitblick …”
- “weil sich Deutschland überall einmischt”
- “reducing national gas production”

## Code: Industry and market actors

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on companies, suppliers, producers, traders, utilities, or market actors whose decisions, strategies, pricing, lobbying, or profit-seeking behavior caused or worsened the crisis.

Inclusion criteria:

- Blames energy companies, utilities, oil and gas firms, or market actors
- Blames pricing behavior, profiteering, speculation, underinvestment, procurement decisions, lobbying, or similar.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only evaluates the crisis response, without clearly presenting that response as causing, worsening, or prolonging it.
- Only blames political actors.
- Describes companies only as affected / victims.
- Only blames capitalism or greed in abstract terms without any concrete company or industry actor.
- Only blames war/Russia without linking to corporate/industry behavior

Included examples:

- “Geldgier der Energieversorger”
- caused by price speculation
- “Energiefirmen … bereichert”
- “Gewinnmaximierung im Ölmarkt”

## Code: Media actors

Description:  
Blames journalists, news papers, media organizations, social media platforms, or media-driven information dynamics that cause/worsen/prolonging the crisis through panic, misinformation, amplification, agenda-setting, or pressure effects.

Inclusion criteria:

- Blames certain news media outlets, media discourse, journalists, or social media to cause drive, worsen, or proling the situation.
- Blames journalists or the general media system for insufficient, inaccurate, or false information.
- Blames informational ignorance/disinformation/rumors, sensationalism, media fear/panic/stockpiling, agenda-setting/pressure effects for hindering the crisis prevention or adequate response.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames media use, radicalization, or polarization as a social problems without blaming media contents or media actors.
- Media is just mentioned as a (neutral) source of information.

Included examples:

- “Medien panik …”
- Die Bildzeitung gehört für diese Hetzkampagne nach wie vor irgendwie bestraft

## Code: Russia's war on Ukraine

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on the war/invasion/attack relating to Russia/Ukraine as a cause.

Inclusion criteria:

- Blames the (current) Ukraine war as the driver of higher prices/shortages.
- Russia/Putin’s wartime actions or sabotage are described as causing the energy crisis.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames high import dependence without war/invasion cues.
- “Wars” or “conflicts” (plural) are mentioned rather generically without (implicit) reference to Ukraine or Russia.
- Only blames sanctions without mentioning or considering Ukraine war/invasion as underlying reason.

Included examples:

- “Wegen des Ukrainekriegs…”
- “Der russische Überfall…”
- "seit dem Krieg ein Engpass"
- “Putin und sein Vorgehen gegen die Ukraine.”

## Code: Too-fast energy transition

Description:
Blames a too-fast or mislead energy transition and other climate/environmental protection efforts for the energy crisis, especially excessive reliance on renewables.

Inclusion criteria:

- Blames renewables, greening, electrification, or the implementation of the energy transition for causing or worsening the crisis.
- Blames steady/increasing demand while reducing fossil energy production capacity;
- Blames costs and problems of renewable energy sources.
- Blames efforts to protect the environmental or climate, Green politics, and other salient sustainability concerns, regulations, and measures.
- The transition is described as too fast, premature, unrealistic, or destabilizing.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames weather or natural conditions without criticizing transition policy.
- Only mentiones renewable energy as a solution but not as the cause.

Included examples:

- “zu viel Wind/Solar …” (when framed as causal)
- "Te snel overstappen van fossiele brandstoffen naar puur electrich"

## Code: Dependence on fossil fuels

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on the general/continued dependence on fossil energy (gas, coal, oil) or the insufficient, hindered, delayed, or too slow transition towards renewable energies (solar, wind, hydro, bio) and related infrastructure (storage, distribution infrastructure, smart grid).

Inclusion criteria:

- Blames dependence on gas, oil, coal, or fossil energy.
- Blames slow transition to renewables, alternative energy sources, slow electrification, or related infrastructure.
- Blames low investments or other political measures that slow the transition to renewables, such as cutting subsidies for PV / wind, storage or distribution infrastructure, or low innovation efforts.
- Blames insufficient efforts to generally reduce fossil energy demands.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames import dependency without general fossil dependence or slow transition critique.
- Only blames war, sanctions, sabotage, or external shocks, without mentioning fossil fuels or import dependence.
- Only blames a too-fast transition away from fossil fuels.
- Only blames high energy prices without criticizing dependence. 

Included examples:

- “Gaslastiges Energiesystem”
- "auf Billig Gas und Öl Preis gesetzt"
- "We waren te afhankelijk van fossiele brandstoffen"
- “lentidão em medidas para mais energias renováveis”
- "abolishing the subsidies for solar panels"
- "neglect of renewable energy sources"

## Code: Energy import dependence vulnerability

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on the dependence on one, few, or *certain* energy suppliers / source countries or on to failure to diversify / reduce energy imports to increase Europe's energy sovereignty.

Inclusion criteria:

- Blames EU or national dependence on imported energy from one or few countries or suppliers.
- Blames ignorance, inaction or delayed action to reduce the vulernability and dependency on *certain* countries.
- Lack of diversification, overexposure to Russian gas/oil/coal, or being too tied to one/few suppliers are presented as causal.
- Blames the lack of alternative import sources.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames Russia's war without mentioning import vulnerability
- Only blames the general fossil energy dependence or too slow transition without critisizing supplier concentration, import dependence, or low self-sufficiency.

Included examples:

- “Die EU hing zu sehr am russischen Gas”
- "Dat we gas moesten halen uit landen zoals Rusland"
- “Russische Gaslieferungen”
- "Verknappung der Energie aus den importländern"
- “Merkel hat sich zu sehr auf Putin verlassen”
- "the great dependence on certain countries"

## Code: Uncontrollable natural disasters

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on natural or environmental events, such as extreme cold, drought, flood, heat, low water levels, other weather-related disruptions, or generally climate change.

Inclusion criteria:

- Blames natural events (weather, earth quakes, etc.) for causing energy shortages, power plant failures, or worsening the crisis.
- Attributed the crisis to climate change.
- The framing is nature/unavoidable rather than policy failure.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames weather fluctioations to frame renewables as unreliable/insufficient energy sources
- Only blames climate ideology or climate policies rather than physical environmental events.
- Only blames Covid-19 or pandemics

Included examples:

- “extremer Winter …”
- "Klimaprobleme"
- “Viele französische Atomkraftwerke waren vom Netz” (due to weather events, not political agenda)

## Code: External shocks and technical failures

Description:  
Blames the energy crisis on external shocks, external actors, or technical failures outside the responsibility of EU or national political actors, such as pandemics, non-Ukraine geopolitical conflicts/wars, global market disruptions, trade restrictions, or infrastructure breakdowns.

Inclusion criteria:

- Blames COVID-19, the pandemic, post-pandemic recovery, or related disruptions.
- Blames non-Ukraine wars, geopolitical conflicts, international tensions, trade restrictions, or political developments involving EU external actors
- Blames actors outside the EU and its member states, such as the USA, China, oil-producing countries, or unnamed foreign powers.
- Blames supply/demand shocks, such as fast rising demand, strong market volatility, gas or oil price surges, or supply disruption.
- Blames grid failure, pipeline damage, power plant breakdown, blocked supplies, or other technical failures.
- The cause is presented as a discrete external event or disruption rather than a long-term structural condition.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames Russia’s war on Ukraine.
- Only blames sanctions, blockades, or deliberate reduction of Russian imports by the EU or member states.
- Only blames EU or national political actors, policies, planning failures, or governance.
- Only blames structural dependence on fossil fuels, imports, or specific suppliers.
- Only blames abstract ideology, morality, or social mentality.
- Only blames natural or environmental disasters.

Included examples:

- “Corona Pandemic …”
- “The sudden surge in gas plus the huge energy demand after the pandemic”
- “The fluctuations of the energy markets that follow political developments, crises or conflicts (Arabia or Ukraine)”
- “the failure of French nuclear power plants”
- “the United States with the taxes imposed”
- “Scarce resources and global politics over many years”

## Code: Abstract societal and ideological explanations

**Description:**
Attributes the energy crisis to abstract ideologies (e.g., green ideology, neoliberalism), system logics/limitations (lack of control), generalized moral or cognitive failings (e.g., irrationality, incompetence, false beliefs, short-term thinking), or broad trends (e.g., growing population/consumption/energy demand) rather than to a specific actor, institution, or event.

Inclusion criteria:

- The cause is abstract, generalized, or diffuse: the explanation would still make sense without naming a specific decision-maker.
- Blames broad trends, such as population growth, consumption, or increasing energy demands.

Exclusion criteria:
- Only blames concrete actors, discrete events, or structural energy mechanism.
- Only attributes ideology, greed, irrationality, or poor planning to a specific actor.
- Only the text sounds ideological but it does itself not blame ideology, abstract failure, or social trends.

Included examples:

- “grüne Ideologie / Klimawahn”
- “Kapitalismus / neoliberale Marktwirtschaft”
- “Gier / Profitlogik”
- “Irrationalität / falsche Vorstellungen”
- “kurzfristiges Denken”
- “das System / der Markt”

## Code: EU sanctions against Russia

Description:  
Blames sanctions against Russia, or politically motivated cuts, stoppages, replacement, or phase-out of Russian oil and gas imports, for causing or worsening the energy crisis.

Inclusion criteria:

- Blames sanctions against Russia for the energy crisis.
- Blames reducing, halting, restricting, banning, replacing, terminating, or ending imports / contracts of Russian oil, gas, or other energy as a political response to the war in Ukraine.
- Blames reducing dependence on Russian energy for the crisis (e.g. finding other sources).
- Includes wording such as blockades against Russia, cutting off from Russian sources, no longer importing Russian oil or gas, or decisions to buy elsewhere, when framed as an intentional political response.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames the war in Ukraine without mentioning sanctions, cutting ties, import bans, or politically motivated reduction of Russian energy purchases.
- Only mentions reduced or disrupted Russian supply which could refer to Russia's own actions or sabotage rather than EU or member-state political decisions.
- Only blames general fossil / energy dependence or import vulnerability without links to sanctions or intentional reduction of Russian imports.
- Mentions reduced Russian imports only as a consequence of the crisis, not as a cause of it.

Included examples:

- “blockades against Russia”
- “sanctions and cutting ties with Russia”
- “the reduction of Russian gas imports”
- “We no longer import Russian oil”
- “After some meetings they chose to buy energy from suppliers other than Russia”
- “Stopping supplies from Russia”
- "A nonsensical attempt to suppress Russia"

## Code: Reduced nuclear or fossil energy production capacity

Description:  
Blames nuclear phase-out or longterm reduced fossil energy production.

Inclusion criteria:

- Blames disfavoring or ignoring nuclear power.
- Blames closing / reducing fossil energy production, including national gas/coal supply chains, such as mines, gas fields, refineries, and national energy plants.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames too slow energy transition
- Only blames low support for renewable energy
- Only blames temporary nuclear shutdowns that are caused by weather, low water levels, or technical conditions rather than policy.

Included examples:

- the reduction of our own gas production
- giving up nuclear energy
- closing of coal mines
- abandoning coal‑based electricity generation

## Code: Insufficient political efforts to make energy affordable

Description:  
Blames insufficient or mislead political efforts to make energy affordable for consumers and industry.

Inclusion criteria:

- Claims that price regulation efforts, levies, or subsidies were insufficient, delayed, or hindered.
- Blames insufficient or false energy market regulation/design for high prices.
- Blames false political priorities or ideologies for insufficient/mislead price regulation or social subsidies, etc.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames market participants (energy providers, stock exchange, importers) but not lack of price regulation efforts or subsidies.
- Only blames greed and injustice in the abstract aside from a political regulation context.

Included examples:

- government's inability to regulate energy prices
- government doesn't help keep a decent price.
- A limited control over those who set energy prices
- Neoliberal profit maximization prioritized well‑being

## Code: Insufficient EU coordination and communication

Description:  
Blames the EU, EU institutions, or the lack of coordination among EU member states for worsening the energy crisis through delayed action, weak coordination, poor communication, inadequate warning, lack of decisive action, inconsistent crisis response, or lack of preparedness.

Inclusion criteria:
- Blames failure by the EU or member states to act together.
- Blames poor communication or coordination between EU member states, e.g., leading to price hikes.
- Blames slow, delayed, inconsistent, or indecisive collective European response to the emerging crisis.
- Blames EU-level actors for unclear communication or ignoring/downplaying signs of the crisis.
- Blames lack of preparedness, funds, planning, or organization, for crisis management at EU or cross-state level.

Exclusion criteria:

- Only blames EU policy substance (e.g. Green Deal, sanctions, or energy transition).
- Only blames single national governments with no European or multi-state framing.
- Only blames market forces, war, oil prices, or Russia but no EU coordination/communication failure.

Included examples:

- The EU's lack of decisive action
- Poor communication of the EU with other states
- The EU's lack of decisive action to become independent from energy supplies
